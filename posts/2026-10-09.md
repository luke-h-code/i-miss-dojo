# The Tip

You’ve reached the bit of autumn when “a quick drink” requires knitwear logistics and every pumpkin looks suspiciously like a business opportunity. This weekend, the clocks go back on Sunday 25 October: one extra hour to squander beautifully. ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/when-do-the-clocks-change?hss_channel=tw-89465662&utm_source=openai)) Before then, you’ve got free art, oddball music, proper pub carbohydrates and dancing that needn’t involve a £40 ticket. Halloween can wait another week. Your out-of-office personality, frankly, has waited long enough.

## The little area guide
### A Peckham Saturday that doesn’t require a small inheritance

You could spend Saturday 24 October crossing London to prove you’re having a weekend. Alternatively, give yourself one patch, three cultural stops and permission not to optimise every waking second.

**Noon: start with the heavy stuff.** Head to South London Gallery for *Weight of Matter*. Give the sculptures time, then look at the paper models: the shift in scale is part of the pleasure. Both gallery buildings are open until 6pm, and neither requires a ticket. You can leave without visiting a gift shop. Be brave. ([southlondongallery.org](https://www.southlondongallery.org/exhibitions/monika-sosnowska-weight-of-matter/))

**Early afternoon: move to Copeland Park.** Peckham Platform’s opening runs from 1–5pm. Explore the new space and passageway artwork, then choose your own level of sociability. There’s a mobile archive for quiet browsing; the 3–4pm creative-careers panel needs a free reservation. Prefer listening without career-related feelings? Orii supplies music from 4–5pm. ([peckhamplatform.com](https://peckhamplatform.com/all-events/where-we-are-now-welcome-to-our-new-space/))

**Afterwards: investigate the digital neighbours.** Peckham Digital’s Saturday programme runs until 10pm, with Copeland Gallery and Rabbet among its venues. Reserve the exhibition or a specific session through the festival’s ticket page rather than assuming every workshop has infinite elbows’ worth of space. ([copelandpark.com](https://www.copelandpark.com/events/40738/peckham-digital-2026/?t=40751))

Your cultural admission bill can be **£0**, excluding travel, food and any voluntary donations. More importantly, you’ve left breathing room. This is a suggested afternoon, not community service with better typography.

## Minted to Skinted
### Same pub, different relationship with payday

For Friday 23 or Saturday 24 October, the Princess of Shoreditch offers a useful experiment: how much “going out for food” do you actually want? Its downstairs pub takes walk-ins; the upstairs Bistro Sablé residency accepts reservations. Same address, two rather different levels of commitment. ([theprincessofshoreditch.com](https://theprincessofshoreditch.com/))

### Skinted: £8 for food

Order the **jambon-beurre baguette downstairs**. That’s the published food price, not a promise that you’ll resist everything else on the blackboard. Keep it as lunch rather than pretending one sandwich is a three-course occasion. There’s dignity in knowing what you came for. ([theprincessofshoreditch.com](https://theprincessofshoreditch.com/))

### Minted-ish: £35.50 for food

Upstairs, **French onion soup (£8.50) followed by coq au vin (£27)** gets you a two-course bistro dinner. It’s a step up in spend without immediately entering “shall we remortgage for the tasting menu?” territory. Book ahead; the dining room isn’t step-free. ([theprincessofshoreditch.com](https://theprincessofshoreditch.com/))

**The difference: £27.50 per person**, before drinks and any service charge. Neither total includes those extras.

The verdict? Choose upstairs when sitting down together is the point. Choose downstairs when you’re hungry and have somewhere else to be. Don’t order the expensive version merely to demonstrate romantic intent: attentive listening remains free, although apparently considerably harder to source.

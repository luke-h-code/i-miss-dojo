# The Tip
## London, Saturday 10–Sunday 11 October 2026
### Art, afters and something to soak it up

You’ve reached the bit of October when leaving the house requires both ambition and a second layer. Fortunately, the London Film Festival is underway and Cecilia Vicuña’s Whitechapel exhibition has just opened, giving you excellent reasons to stop watching people cook dinner on your phone. ([bfi.org.uk](https://www.bfi.org.uk/london-film-festival/news/full-programme-announced-70th-bfi-london-film-festival?utm_source=openai)) This weekend, you can graduate from woolly installations to sweaty dancefloors, support refugee-owned food businesses and finish with free jazz. Staying in remains respectable. Doing it accidentally because the group chat couldn’t decide is bollocks.

*Prices and listings checked on Friday 9 October. Where live seat or table inventory wasn’t visible, that’s flagged below. Free entry doesn’t mean free dinner, sadly.*

## Some events for doing

### 1. Cecilia Vicuña: Living Threads
**Whitechapel Gallery**

Your knitwear has competition. Vicuña’s suspended wool installation *Quipu Menstrual* sits within six decades of art, poetry and activism. Pick this for something more nourishing than another exhibition designed principally to improve your profile picture.

**Go:** Sat–Sun, 11am–6pm. 77–82 Whitechapel High Street, E1 7QX. £16.50, concessions £9.50. Booking offered online, live inventory not visible. [Get yourself in a productive tangle →](https://www.whitechapelgallery.org/exhibitions/cecilia-vicuna/) ([whitechapelgallery.org](https://www.whitechapelgallery.org/exhibitions/cecilia-vicuna/))

### 2. The Other Art Fair
**The Truman Brewery**

You don’t need a country house to look at original art. More than 130 independent artists, installations and a bar make this a promising alternative to gallery-door intimidation. Looking is enough. Your landlord’s magnolia can wait.

**Go:** Sat 11am–7pm, Sun 11am–6pm. 83 Brick Lane, E1 6QL. £22 including fees. Online and door sales, subject to availability. [Develop expensive taste without immediately acting on it →](https://www.theotherartfair.com/london/tickets/) ([theotherartfair.com](https://www.theotherartfair.com/london/tickets/))

### 3. The Banality of Evil: Hannah Arendt, France, 1940
**Arcola Theatre**

Give your brain something tougher than remembering passwords. Julia Pascal’s play follows three German Jewish women imprisoned in France, mixing cabaret, satire and physical theatre. Saturday’s post-show discussion makes this the thinking-person’s pick, not a light distraction.

**Go:** Sat, 7pm, followed by Griselda Pollock and Pascal in conversation. 24 Ashwin Street, E8 3DL. Advertised range £15–£29. Saturday availability unconfirmed, call **020 7503 1646** before travelling. Ages 16+, check content warnings. [Exercise something above the neck →](https://www.arcolatheatre.com/event/the-banality-of-evil/) ([arcolatheatre.com](https://www.arcolatheatre.com/event/the-banality-of-evil/))

### 4. Once Upon a Time in Harlem
**BFI Southbank**

Your fantasy dinner party has been comprehensively outclassed. William Greaves filmed Harlem Renaissance figures gathered at Duke Ellington’s townhouse, with his son David completing the documentary decades later. Choose this for cultural history with actual people in it.

**Go:** Sun, 6.15pm, NFT3, BFI Southbank, SE1 8XT. £18 standard, plus £1 online booking fee for non-members. Live availability requires the booking system. If full, join the Southbank standby queue 30 minutes before. [Accept Ellington’s considerably better invitation →](https://whatson.bfi.org.uk/lff/Online/default.asp?BOparam%3A%3AWScontent%3A%3AloadArticle%3A%3Apermalink=once-upon-a-time-in-harlem-film-lff26) ([whatson.bfi.org.uk](https://whatson.bfi.org.uk/lff/Online/default.asp?BOparam%3A%3AWScontent%3A%3AloadArticle%3A%3Apermalink=once-upon-a-time-in-harlem-film-lff26))

## Minted to Skinted
### Same Peckham dancefloor, different damage

You do not need two different lifestyles. You need one venue and the ability to distinguish “I fancy that” from “I’m buying that because someone else ordered it”.

Saturday’s **Dinner to Dance at Multi Story** gives you the useful constant: K.Wilson, house and UK garage, and no entry charge. The food and drink choices below are taken from the published menu, not imaginary meal deals negotiated by your newsletter’s enormous commercial muscle. ([multistorypeckham.com](https://www.multistorypeckham.com/events/dinner-to-dance-kwilson))

### Skinted: £17

Order the **£12 margherita-style pizza** and **£5 house lager**. You’ve bought dinner and a drink, with the dancefloor still costing nothing. That is the plan. Resist adding three small plates because you’ve decided the pizza needs emotional support. ([multistorypeckham.com](https://www.multistorypeckham.com/food-drinks))

### Minted: £34

Take the **£22 full rack of ribs with chips** and a **£12 Multi Story Margarita**. Twice the menu spend, exactly the same DJ. You’re paying for your preferred dinner, not a superior class of Saturday night. There is something reassuringly democratic about everyone eventually dancing like they’re drying themselves with a towel. ([multistorypeckham.com](https://www.multistorypeckham.com/food-drinks))

**The verdict:** pick the food you actually want, decide your drinks budget before arriving and stop treating the cheapest viable evening as a personal failure. These totals exclude transport, additional orders and any service charge.

## Dating 101
### Sunday afternoon, without the seven-hour hostage situation

You want a date, not a residential assessment centre. For **Sunday 11 October**, build around one reservation and one optional extension.

**1. Start with lunch, not a vague promise to “wander”.**  
Book The Cambria for around 12.30pm, subject to availability. Its £21 roast choices include meat and a vegan Wellington, so you can establish dietary requirements without conducting a compatibility tribunal. Two roasts come to £42 before drinks, extras and any service charge. ([thecambrialondon.co.uk](https://thecambrialondon.co.uk/food-drink/?utm_source=openai))

**2. Make the second act genuinely optional.**  
If you’re getting on, suggest Ruskin Park’s free South London Jazz Orchestra performance, **3–5pm**. The Cambria is close to the park, making this a practical continuation rather than a cross-city transfer disguised as romance. ([camra.org.uk](https://camra.org.uk/pubs/cambria-camberwell-158780?utm_source=openai))

**3. Don’t turn the band into an exam.**  
You do not need to identify the saxophone player’s influences. “I like this” is a complete sentence. So is “I’m a bit cold”. Bring a layer and leave room for either of you to call it an afternoon.

**4. End while you still fancy each other.**  
There is no prize for stretching a good two hours into six increasingly administrative ones. If the chemistry’s there, arrange another date. If it isn’t, you’ve had lunch and heard some music. Nobody needs to file a report.

[Arrange a little sax, without making it weird →](https://www.hernehillfestival.org/events/2026-10-11-ruskin-park-afternoon)
